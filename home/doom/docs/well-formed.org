#+title: well-formed
#+subtitle: A writing assistant made of rules, with a model on top
#+texinfo_filename: well-formed.info
#+texinfo_dir_category: This configuration
#+texinfo_dir_title: well-formed: (well-formed)
#+texinfo_dir_desc: The writing assistant

well-formed reads what you write as you write it, the way Grammarly does,
in English and German: mistakes, unclear sentences, worn phrases, tone, and
whether a document stays consistent with itself.  Its ground floor is
rules and statistics in Emacs Lisp, with no server and no network; above it,
optionally, a language model reads the paragraphs you change.

It checks Org, Markdown, plain text, commit and mail messages, in files and
in buffers that aren't files (a capture, a scratch buffer).  In code it
reads only comments, and only if you turn it on there.

* Reading the marks

Findings are underlined like any other diagnostic and listed by flycheck.
Under the cursor, the echo area says what's wrong, the rule and the source:

: ● Use “an” before “apple”  well-formed · article · SPC P x fixes

Red is a mistake (correctness), yellow something to reconsider, the quiet
ones are suggestions.  =]e= and =[e= go to the next and previous one.

* The keys

| Key       | Does                                                            |
|-----------+-----------------------------------------------------------------|
| =SPC P x= | fix the finding at point (a choice when there are several)      |
| =SPC P X= | fix one rule throughout the buffer, asking for each             |
| =SPC P g= | ignore it: here, this rule in this document, or everywhere      |
| =SPC P e= | what the rule is about, with examples                           |
| =SPC P L= | every finding, grouped, previewed as you move                   |
| =SPC P v= | the report: scores, readability, tone, plots                    |
| =SPC P a= | the model's candid assessment of the whole text                 |
| =SPC P S= | the style for this buffer                                       |
| =SPC P m= | all of it in one menu                                           |
| =SPC t m= | reading mode: every mark hidden, back as it was afterwards      |

* Fixes

Every finding can be fixed:

- the word or phrase to use instead (=in order to= → =to=);
- for a long or hard sentence, where it splits best, at a clause boundary
  (=…, and the team= → =…. The team=), the most balanced split first;
- for a passive with its actor, the active form (=The file was deleted by
  the script= → =The script deleted the file=);
- and always, "Rewrite the sentence with AI…": the model rewrites the
  sentence toward what the finding says, and a popup shows the change word
  by word; =C-c C-c= applies it, =g= asks again, =q= keeps yours.

* Styles

A style changes what is reported and how strictly: sentence length limits,
whether contractions, informal words, the first person or passive voice
count.

| Style       | Long sentence | Notes                                          |
|-------------+---------------+------------------------------------------------|
| general     | 30 words      | the default                                    |
| academic    | 40 words      | contractions and informal words reported       |
| technical   | 30 words      | hedges and unsourced claims reported           |
| business    | 25 words      | jargon and negative framing reported           |
| casual      | 45 words      | little more than mistakes                      |
| creative    | no limit      | clichés and adverbs reported                   |

A document keeps its style in its first lines:

: #+well_formed: academic

For the model you can say anything it will understand:

: #+well_formed_goal: a friendly README for Emacs users who know Lisp

* What it checks

** Correctness
Repeated words; =a=/=an= by sound (/an hour/, /a university/, /an SQL query/);
spacing and doubled punctuation (two spaces after a sentence are fine);
capitals at sentence starts, /I/, days and months; words confused for one
that sounds alike (then/than, its/it's, your/you're, could of); subject and
verb agreement; a comma after /However/; English as German speakers write
it (/since three years/, /informations/, /explain me/); names as their
owners spell them (GitHub, macOS, NixOS); typography (a hyphen used as a
dash, three dots).

German: /das/ and /dass/, /seit/ and /seid/, the comma before /dass/,
/weil/, /obwohl/, common misspellings a spell checker lets through
(/Standart/, /wiederspiegeln/), /Sinn machen/, the genitive after /wegen/.

Spelling itself is jinx's (=z==), shown in the report too.

** Clarity
Wordy phrases and their short form, in English and German (Nominalstil:
/eine Entscheidung treffen/ → /entscheiden/); redundant pairs (/end
result/, /bereits schon/); plainer words (/utilize/ → /use/); long and hard
sentences; passive voice; double negatives; a bare /This shows/; sentences
held together by too many commas or /and/s.

** Engagement
Clichés and German Floskeln; /very/ with a plain word (/very important/ →
/crucial/); a word used three times in a paragraph; three sentences in a row
starting alike; clusters of adverbs.

** Delivery
Hedges and Füllwörter; informal words and contractions in formal styles;
jargon; inclusive language; claims without a source (/studies show/);
shouting; negative framing (business); the first person (academic).

** Consistency
Per document, the more frequent form wins: American or British spelling,
/email/ or /e-mail/, the serial comma, straight or curly quotes.

* The model's reading

On by default.  When you pause, each paragraph you changed (only those,
once per text, at most six a minute) goes to the fast model with the
document's style or goal.  It reports only what rules can't: a sentence that
doesn't say what you mean, wording that misses the style or audience,
inconsistent terms, Org used wrongly.  It quotes your text exactly, so the
finding sits where it belongs, and offers its rewrite as the fix.  Its
findings carry the rule =ai= and can be ignored like any other.

=SPC P a= asks the stronger model for an assessment of the whole text: how
it leans (percentages of styles it names), tone, audience, how it fits your
declared goal, ratings with reasons, strengths, the issues that matter most,
and the consistency of names and Org markup.  It is told to be candid: no
praise that isn't earned.

* The report

=SPC P v= shows, for the buffer or the region: a score per category; words,
sentences, reading, speaking and writing time (the time you spent editing
the file); readability (Flesch, Flesch–Kincaid grade, Gunning fog, SMOG,
Coleman–Liau, ARI, LIX, and the Wiener Sachtextformel for German); the tone
mix from word statistics; sentence lengths through the text and their
histogram; reading ease per paragraph; findings by rule; the words you use
most.  Every bar and line goes to its place in the text.

* How it stays fast

It masks what isn't prose first (Org and Markdown markup, code, links,
tables, drawers, blocks; quote blocks too, since what you quote isn't your
writing), cuts the text into paragraphs and checks each once: an edit
re-checks only the paragraph it touched.  Checking runs when you pause and
stops as soon as you press a key; the buffer is checked again a moment
later.

* Configuring

| Variable                           | Meaning                                         |
|------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------|
| =well-formed-style=                | the default style                               |
| =well-formed-language=             | force "en" or "de" (default: per paragraph)    |
| =well-formed-disabled-rules=       | rules never reported                            |
| =well-formed-disabled-categories=  | categories never reported                       |
| =well-formed-check-quotes=         | check quote blocks too                          |
| =well-formed-modes=                | major modes it turns on in                      |
| =well-formed-ai-max-per-minute=    | the model's request budget                      |

Rules are data: their word lists are variables (=well-formed-wordy-en=,
=well-formed-redundant-de=, …), and =well-formed-define-rule= adds one.
